# Middle Worldbuilding

## The idea

Three scales:

**Macro** — wars, gods, kingdoms, ancient history  
**Middle** — how society actually functions because of those things  
**Micro** — individual people, places, objects

DragonSword is unusually good at the **middle**.

## Write the sequel

Use:

**premise → consequence → adaptation → normalization → culture → story**

Example:

**Monsters exist** is lore.

But the sequel is:

- merchants hire escorts;
- roads become dangerous in known places;
- villages maintain guards;
- children learn which creatures to avoid;
- mercenary work becomes an industry.

That is middle worldbuilding.

## Why Orbis works

The warm art direction shows:

- farms
- roads
- ordinary settlements
- merchants
- food
- local errands
- companionship
- ruins embedded in inhabited land

So the world feels **worth saving**, not already dead.

## Questions to keep asking

- Who maintains roads?
- Who hires mercenaries?
- Who regulates magic?
- Who owns ruins/artifacts?
- What authority does the Organa Order really have?
- How do ordinary people remember the Six Heroes?
- What social systems grew around Dragon Dust?
- What precautions are so normal nobody explains them?

The hidden world often lives in the **consequences**, not the exposition.
