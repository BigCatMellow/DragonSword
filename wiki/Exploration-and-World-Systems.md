# Exploration and World Systems

## General rule

If something interesting is nearby:

**usually investigate it**

DragonSword ties exploration into progression more than it first appears.

## Worth touching

- waypoints;
- dungeon entrances;
- puzzles;
- chests;
- unusual ruins;
- treasure-map clues;
- mounts/familiars;
- cooking locations/resources.

## Puzzles

Puzzles are not only side distractions.

They can feed:

- treasure maps;
- progression materials;
- later skill/Awakening-related resources;
- hidden lore.

See [[Puzzles]] when stuck.

## Dungeon entrances

Activate them as you pass.

Later:

**material needed → reward list → fast travel → farm**

## Unreachable places

Do not assume every ledge/underwater area has an immediate route.

Some require later traversal abilities.

Useful mental note:

> “probably later” is sometimes the correct answer.

## Ruins and environmental clues

For gameplay:

- murals can become later puzzle answers;
- damaged walls may hide mechanisms;
- elevation matters;
- rooms often reuse visual clues.

For lore:

- statues;
- watchtowers;
- old roads;
- abandoned machines;
- sealed spaces;
- ruins beside modern settlements

may be evidence about older layers of Orbis.

See [[Orbis Iceberg]].

## Mounts / familiars

Collecting them can matter beyond appearance.

Think:

**travel + access + progression**

rather than only cosmetic mounts.

## Cooking

Mark useful cooking spots mentally/in notes if the map does not make them obvious.

Ingredients are worth picking up because food is genuinely useful later.

## World bosses / optional activities

Do not assume “optional” means irrelevant.

Some are efficient sources for otherwise annoying progression materials.

## Exploration habit

When entering a ruin:

1. look at walls/murals before interacting;
2. note cracked/damaged surfaces;
3. look up and down;
4. activate travel points;
5. check reward/source descriptions;
6. screenshot anything that looks like a code/pattern.

That solves a surprising number of later “what am I supposed to do?” moments.
